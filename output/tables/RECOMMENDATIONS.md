# Recommendations for NovaCorp's CHRO

## Prioritisation logic

Rank by (a) $ exposure, (b) tractability/speed to impact, (c) headcount concentration
(small, targetable cohorts first). Sequenced as a 90-day / 2-quarter / ongoing roadmap
so the CHRO leaves the room with something actionable "by Monday."

## Recommendation 1 (start Monday, $0 incremental cost): Deploy a disengagement early-warning trigger using data NovaCorp already owns

- Flag any employee whose engagement-survey response rate drops below 60% (completes
  fewer than 3 of the last 5 waves) for an automatic manager check-in within 2 weeks.
  This single rule identifies a population that voluntarily exits at 3x the rate of
  high responders (21.2% vs 6.8%) — a stronger predictor than the survey scores
  themselves.
- Zero new data collection, zero new survey. Pilot in Corporate Operations, Risk &
  Compliance, and Insurance first — the three departments with the highest
  concentration of sustained disengagement (30-37% of headcount).
- Success measure: reduction in the bottom-decile disengaged population (currently
  1,156 people, ~$25M/year exposure) over the next 2 survey waves.

## Recommendation 2 (this quarter): Entity_B Leadership Reconnection Programme

- Root cause is not compensation (Entity_B is paid at or above the company average). It
  is a collapse in trust toward senior leadership (3.05 vs 3.38 company average) and
  purpose/meaning connection (3.06 vs 3.38) — while local managers score fine.
- Action: a senior-leadership (ELT) roadshow and listening-tour series specifically for
  the Entity_B cohort (1,884 people, prioritising the 354 high-value staff), timed
  ahead of the Q2 FY2026 systems/cultural integration completion already committed to
  in the Annual Report.
- Success measure: Entity_B `senior_leadership_trust` and `purpose_meaning` scores move
  toward the company average within 2 survey waves; Entity_B voluntary attrition trends
  from 15.0% toward the ~10% company average within 12 months.
- Financial case: normalising Entity_B high-value attrition to the company average
  protects an estimated **~$5.6M/year** in replacement cost.

## Recommendation 3 (this quarter, in parallel): Critical Regulatory Talent Retention track for Risk & Compliance Director+

- This is a small (44-person), highly specific, and highly paid cohort being poached by
  the external market under FAR-driven demand for regulatory talent — not a pay or
  engagement failure internally.
- Action: external market compensation benchmarking for this specific band, paired with
  accelerated recognition/succession pathways and a knowledge-transfer safety net given
  the flight-risk concentration of deep institutional knowledge.
- Financial case: protects **~$2.6M/year** in direct replacement cost, plus mitigates an
  unquantified but material regulatory/compliance knowledge-continuity risk.

## Quick win (fund the above): Redirect discretionary agency hiring to direct/referral channels

- Agency-sourced hires cost 18% of salary in fees vs a $5,500 direct-hire benchmark, with
  no speed advantage (~51-53 days-to-fill across all channels) and a slightly higher
  early-exit rate.
- Redirecting feasible discretionary hires away from agency saves an estimated
  **~$2.3M/year** in fees alone — enough to fund the manager-training component of
  Recommendation 2 and 3 without new budget.

## What we are not recommending, and why

- We are not recommending a company-wide pay increase. Compensation does not explain
  either of the two attrition root causes we identified with the strongest evidence.
- We are not recommending a "fix bad managers" programme. Manager-level attrition
  clustering was tested directly and found statistically weak (correlation ≈ 0 between
  team engagement and team attrition rate) — the data does not support this narrative.
